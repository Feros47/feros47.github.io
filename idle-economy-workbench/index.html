Idle economy pacing / A reproducible example

One cost change.
One missed budget.

Changing a seed bed's cost-growth factor from 1.16 to 1.20 moves the 1,000-coin production milestone from about 293.8 seconds to 312 seconds. Here is the purchase decision behind that shift.

01 / Isolate the change

Higher costs also change
which purchase comes next.

The baseline is the original Cozy Garden example. Its candidate changes only generators[0].costGrowth, from 1.16 to 1.20. Both start with 20 coins, 0.5 coins per second of base income, no owned units, and a 1,800-second horizon. All generator income, caps, milestones and other inputs remain identical.

The seed bed has a base cost of 20 coins and adds 0.5 coins per second per owned unit. Both scenarios use best-payback: choose the available positive-income unit with the smallest next cost divided by its added income, then wait until it is affordable.

next seed-bed cost = 20 × costGrowthownedpayback seconds = next unit cost ÷ added income per second

Both runs buy a seed bed at time zero, a second seed bed shortly afterward, then an orchard. At that point, two seed beds are owned. The next greenhouse costs 1,400 coins and adds 25 coins per second, giving it a 56-second payback ratio.

The candidate waits for the greenhouse. The policy does not filter its choices to units affordable right now. The baseline buys another seed bed at about 135.85 seconds, raising income to 5 coins per second. The candidate remains at 4.5 coins per second until its greenhouse purchase at about 441.78 seconds. That difference matters before the 1,000-coin milestone.

Check the milestone arithmetic

After its third seed bed, the baseline has earned 210.112 coins at time 135.84711111111113 seconds and produces 5 coins per second. Its crossing time is 135.84711111111113 + (1000 − 210.112) ÷ 5.

After its first orchard, the candidate has earned 184 coins at time 130.66666666666669 seconds and produces 4.5 coins per second. Its crossing time is 130.66666666666669 + (1000 − 184) ÷ 4.5 = 312. Neither run buys a greenhouse before reaching this milestone.

Both JSON files retain the same scenario name so that their only data difference is the cost-growth factor. The files and their concise walkthrough are also available in the free GitHub companion, under an MIT license.

Keep the question narrow.

This example asks whether a stated model produces an amount by a deadline. It does not determine a desirable session length or establish that a game is fun.

Greedy strategies are assumptions. Best payback and lowest cost each choose a target and wait. Neither searches all purchase sequences or predicts player behavior.

Unsupported mechanics need another model. Prestige, prerequisites, multiple currencies, variable clicking sessions and random rewards are outside this release. Production is continuous; arithmetic uses floating-point numbers. Watch purchase caps and stopping reasons, then use playtesting to assess the experience.

Optional: keep the deadline in your build.

The 149 DKK developer kit adds the ready CLI and reusable engine source for local automation. With that downloaded kit and Node.js 20 or newer, --max-milestone-seconds 1000:300 makes the baseline pass and the candidate exit with code 2. The free demo supplies the same browser inspection features; purchase is optional for this tutorial. A maximum-time check can also pass when progress is faster than intended.
